A forest needs more than a bridge.

A strategy game, not a crossing contest

Your first decisions

Time begins paused. Try +1 week to inspect each change, or Start to run. At 1×, eight real seconds advance one simulated week. Each higher speed halves the wait, up to 32×.

  1. Build trust and evidence. Community outreach builds trust; field monitoring builds grant evidence. Each program has a setup cost, recurring bill, minimum commitment, fixed run, and rest period. A running program must be paid to have an effect.
  2. Raise funds before reserves run out. One fundraising team runs one campaign at a time. Awards take time; community turnout and trust affect most event payouts. Grants require 20 evidence and 45 trust both at application and review.
  3. Protect a cash reserve. The top bar shows support minus bills each week. Budget → Details itemizes costs, explains the support formula, and reconciles the last settled week. Setup fees, construction, repairs, and awards are separate ledger entries—not weekly income.
  4. Connect, then restore. Select Negotiate and click a named map route. After a 12-week agreement, build a bridge (16 funded weeks). Plant native cover only after the bridge is open, during the first 22 weeks of the year. Trees mature after 208 funded weeks with adequate survival.
  5. Prepare for the next threat. Threat watch shows the next scheduled event and response. Veterinary visits build vaccination gradually; fire teams help only while funded. Nursery care prioritizes two plantings. Inspect damage and repair before a bridge closes below 45% condition.
  6. Make room for recovery. Plantings increase habitat capacity as they grow. Isolation, crowding, low health, unpaid care, and threats reduce recovery. The right panel explains natural change and threat deaths. Reach 10,000 to win; falling strictly below 1,000 ends the campaign.

No mystery money

Understand the grant gap.

A grant pays its first installment after a 16-week review. The rest waits at least 13 more weeks and four new paid monitoring weeks after the first installment. Then a 52-week renewal cooldown begins.

That is a minimum of 81 weeks from one application to the next. Bills continue the whole time. Grants can help build a corridor, but recurring supporters and careful timing keep it running.

Support comes from trust above 35, with additional mature-corridor support when trust reaches 65. Exact multipliers, rounding, and current inputs are visible in Budget → Details. There are no automatic cash rescues.

A useful starting plan

Start outreach and monitoring, organize a fundraiser while reserves are healthy, and build toward grant eligibility. Complete the grant report before turning monitoring off. Expand one planted corridor at a time, rather than negotiating and building everywhere at once.

Read the map

Inspect any grove for population, health, and vaccination coverage. Inspect a named route for its agreement, bridge condition, and tree survival. Select a tool, then click the route itself; action feedback stays below the map.

Step through, don’t rush

Pause whenever you need. The speed controls, mission, and tutorial guidance sit beneath the map. Opening the budget, player panel, or leaderboard pauses time.

Players & leaderboard

Choose a nickname for a separate local campaign. Finished runs appear on this device’s leaderboard, separated by difficulty. Wins rank by shortest recovery time, then remaining credits; tutorial results stay in their own practice category.

No sign-in or email is needed. You can explicitly share a finished result on the online leaderboard under a nickname. Adults can separately choose to leave a private email and give permission for future project updates. Email deduplicates contact records; it never identifies a public score. No codes or messages are sent automatically. Online scores are unverified guest submissions.

A model, not a forecast

Yellow fever, habitat fragmentation, and conservation care are real concerns. This game’s population targets, costs, rates, schedules, and staffing limits are invented teaching settings. It does not predict actual tamarin population change or convert credits into donations.

An easier first campaign

Time to explore, not a race to bankruptcy.

Explorer starts with 12,000 Forest Credits and a standing sponsor worth 130 FC every week. At the opening trust level, total support is 140 FC against 58 FC in weekly bills: an 82 FC surplus before new projects. The top bar and Budget details show this income explicitly. Sponsor support continues in existing Explorer saves too.

At 1×, all unguided modes advance one week every eight real seconds. Higher speeds are available up to 32×. Pause or step a week at a time whenever you need. Start outreach, try one bridge, and plant its corridor before expanding. More projects and programs still bring recurring costs.

A landscape you can explore

Drag or swipe to pan; scroll or pinch to move from the landscape overview to individual tree crowns, rope rails, and bridge decking. Grove names and route captions keep the same readable size. Hover or tap a route to highlight it and reveal its name; leave the route or tap the landscape to dismiss it. No label boxes cover the forest.

From seedlings to a connected canopy

Planting grows from small seedlings into a wider mixture of illustrated embaúba, jequitibá and yellow ipê trees. These are native-forest examples documented by Programa Arboretum and re.green. The silhouettes, flowering, four-year canopy progression, and bridge sway are simplified game illustrations—not species-identification tools, planting prescriptions, or engineering guidance.
