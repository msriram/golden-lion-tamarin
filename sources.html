Claim guide

What we say—and what we do not.

  • Say: “The 2023 census estimated about 4,800.”
  • Say: “A suitable crossing may help targeted wildlife, and use must be monitored.”
  • Say: “Golden lion tamarins disperse seeds and serve as a flagship and umbrella species.”
  • Do not say: “The species is safe now.”
  • Do not say: “One monkey holds the whole forest together.”
  • Do not say: “Money raised equals field outcomes” without recipient reporting.

Verified

Supported by a relevant, dated source for the geography and claim being made.

Qualified

Evidence exists, but scope, method, age, or geography requires a caveat.

Hypothesis

A classroom design or simulation idea to test—not an approved field solution.

Unknown

We have not found enough reliable evidence yet.

Corrections policy

When the evidence changes, the website changes.

Science improves through better questions, new fieldwork, and corrections. Updating a claim is a sign that the process is working.

  1. Record the concern

    Identify the exact page, wording, source, and date.

  2. Check primary evidence

    Prefer original research, official organizations, and methodologically comparable data.

  3. Make a scoped correction

    Update the claim, caveat, source link, and review date together.

  4. Keep a short changelog

    Note meaningful numerical, status, partnership, or fundraising corrections.

Open before launch

Confirm team name, school permissions, recipient organization, impact equivalencies, photo and logo rights, current field priorities, and whether a newer comparable census exists.
