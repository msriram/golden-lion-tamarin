45-minute lesson

The Forest Connection Challenge

  1. Notice & wonder • 5 minutes

    Show the hero illustration. Ask: “What might make it hard for a tree-living family to cross this landscape?”

  2. Build a model • 10 minutes

    Use green paper, blocks, or classroom objects for two forest patches. Add a paper road and ask groups to create at least two connection ideas.

  3. Play & observe • 15 minutes

    Use the game in pairs. Students record population changes, habitat damage, and why an action was accepted or blocked. Compare an unmanaged forest with a funded restoration plan; explain why bridges alone cannot recover the population.

  4. Compare designs • 10 minutes

    Discuss short canopy crossings, planted corridors, overpasses, and underpasses. Which wildlife groups might each help?

  5. Exit ticket • 5 minutes

    Complete: “A connection is more than a structure because…” and “One question I still have is…”

Learning goals

Explain fragmentation, compare solutions, use evidence to qualify a claim, and identify why community permission matters.

Materials

Device optional. Paper, blocks, string, sticky notes, pencils, and printed map cards work for an unplugged version.

Proposed standards links

NGSS 3-LS4-4, 4-ESS3-2, and 4-ETS1-1/2/3 may be relevant. Teachers should verify local grade placement and current wording.

Discussion cards

Questions with more than one useful answer.

Use these before or after play. Strong answers explain tradeoffs and uncertainty.

Biology

Why might a larger population still be at risk?

Look for: separated forest blocks, disease, genetic isolation, and uneven habitat quality.

Engineering

When is a bridge the wrong tool?

Look for: long gaps, ground animals, missing anchors, permits, maintenance, or a route better served by restored habitat.

Community

Why can’t players plant anywhere they want?

Look for: land rights, livelihoods, consent, local knowledge, long-term care, and practical constraints.

Evidence

What proves a corridor “worked”?

Look for: survival of plantings, canopy readiness, observed use, safe movement, breeding or settlement evidence, and ongoing monitoring.

Four-choice check

Which statement is strongest?

  1. A rope bridge saves every Atlantic Forest animal.
  2. Planting trees creates mature habitat immediately.
  3. A suitable, monitored connection may help targeted wildlife move between habitat patches.
  4. A higher total population means conservation work is finished.
3

Best answer

Choice 3 is careful about suitability, evidence, and which species a connection is designed to help.

Privacy & safety

Play without an account.

No sign-in, email code, analytics, camera access, or location tracking. Every visitor can play without sharing an email.

  • Nicknames, separate campaign saves, and this-device results stay in your browser. Use a nickname, not a full name or email.
  • At the end of a campaign, you can choose Share result online. Only the nickname and game result become public; no email or contact record is attached. These are unverified guest results.
  • Adults may optionally leave their own email in the Player panel to hear from Rainforest Rangers about Tamarin Trails updates, activities, and ways to help. Adult confirmation and outreach permission are separate, unchecked boxes. This is not required to play or share a result.
  • Emails are stored privately in Supabase, deduplicated by trimmed/lowercase address, and never placed in browser saves, the leaderboard, or public contact lists. No email is sent automatically and no ownership verification is performed. Organizers must review unverified interest records before following up.
  • The Stop future contact form suppresses further outreach without revealing whether an address is on the list. A private suppression record prevents accidental re-addition; full erasure needs the team’s adult organizer. Anyone knowing an address could suppress it because this system has no account authentication.
  • Reset replaces the current local campaign but keeps completed results. The Player panel can delete local profiles, saves, and local scores; this does not remove server records. Team information and photos are shared by the organizer; no exact endangered-wildlife locations are published.
