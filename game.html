    Survival guide

    More forest. Fewer losses. A future for tamarins.

    1. Fund the people first

      Outreach builds trust; monitoring creates grant evidence. Each program has its own commitments and rest periods. A grant takes time and requires a report before its final payment.

    2. Bridge, then grow

      Negotiate with landowners and build a bridge before planting nearby. Young trees gradually widen into forest corridors over four years. More cover provides space for a larger population; crossings alone do not create habitat.

    3. Prepare before trouble arrives

      Specialist veterinary teams gradually vaccinate tamarins. Paid fire teams reduce fire damage; outreach softens construction pressure. Nursery care reaches only two planting areas at once. Watch alerts and budget for each response.

    4. Keep habitat healthy

      Unmanaged forest shrinks. Hungry, isolated populations decline, especially when bills go unpaid. Damaged bridges close below 45% condition; damaged trees need replanting. Mature corridors help the surrounding forest recover.

    The conservation goal

    Start with 5,000 tamarins. Win at 10,000. Below 1,000, the campaign ends. Running out of money does not immediately end play—but unpaid care accelerates habitat damage and population loss.

    Read your landscape

    Inspect a grove for its population, health, habitat capacity, and vaccination coverage. Burned or cleared groves shrink and lose trees. Green cover along planted routes expands as seedlings grow.

    Grounded in field practice

    Yellow fever and fragmented habitat are real threats. Restoration and grant reporting take time. Event schedules, population targets, costs, and biological rates are invented for this game—not predictions or field measurements.
